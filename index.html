The Neighborhood Story

Life at 825

LIFE

Life here runs on its own rhythm. Mornings start with coffee on the deck, the city skyline just waking up across the water, before a lakeside walk or run around Green Lake, six-tenths of a mile away and one of Seattle's best-loved loops. Afternoons might mean a wander through Cowen Park's ravine trails, a stroller loop around the Maple Leaf Reservoir, or a coffee run to one of the neighborhood's espresso spots. When the sun starts to dip, Roosevelt's small business district is a five-minute walk: organic breakfast at Portage Bay Cafe, fresh-baked pastries at Macrina Bakery & Cafe, brunch and bar food at The Maple, a slice at Olympic Pizza, or sushi at Sushi Tokyo. It's a neighborhood built for actually living in, not just passing through.

THE COMMUTE

This is about as centrally located as Seattle gets. I-5's Roosevelt/Ravenna exit is minutes away, putting downtown Seattle roughly fifteen minutes south with no surface streets required. Heading east, SR 520 is a straight shot from I-5, making the Eastside, Microsoft, Bellevue, Redmond, a realistic reverse commute. Headed north, that same freeway access puts Northgate, Shoreline, and Everett all within easy reach. And on car-free days, the Roosevelt light rail station is just a half-mile walk, with direct service to the University of Washington, Capitol Hill, Downtown, and Sea-Tac Airport. For a walkable in-city neighborhood, that kind of rail access is rare, and it's one of the biggest reasons this location works for almost any commute.

THE SCHOOLS

Roosevelt High School, rated 10 out of 10 by GreatSchools, is a half-mile walk from the front door, and Eckstein Middle School, rated 9 out of 10 by GreatSchools, sits on the same street, just over a mile east. The assigned elementary is Green Lake Elementary, and Thornton Creek, an open-enrollment K-5 school, is also open to students citywide for families who want to explore Seattle Public Schools' additional program options.

About This Home

825 NE 75th St, Seattle, WA 98115

Perched above what locals call Banner Island in Seattle's Roosevelt neighborhood, this 1909 craftsman was substantially rebuilt in 1984 and has been meticulously re-crafted inside and out since, with custom built-ins on every level and a light-filled floor plan that still carries all its original character. Green Lake is two blocks away, the Roosevelt Link light rail station is a half-mile walk with direct service to the U District, Capitol Hill, Downtown Seattle, and Sea-Tac Airport, and the shops and restaurants of Roosevelt, Maple Leaf, and Wallingford are all within easy reach: rare in-city access without giving up a quiet, residential street. The main floor pairs a fireplace-and-built-in living room with a formal dining room and an eat-in kitchen; upstairs, a second family room with its own fireplace opens through French doors to a view deck looking out over the city and the mountains.

The vaulted primary suite, capped with an exposed beam and a wall of windows, is the showpiece, but there's plenty of room to spread out and plenty of places to put things away: a second bedroom, a home office, two full baths, a partially finished lower level with a guest room, rec space, and its own dedicated wine cellar, a dedicated shop off the attached garage, and closet and storage space throughout that's genuinely rare for a home this age. Out front, a long, secluded driveway and terraced planting beds lead up to the covered porch; out back, a secluded, professionally landscaped garden offers a quiet retreat of its own.

For more than ten of those years, 825 NE 75th St has been our home. It started as a house with a long list of renovation projects and ideas for what it could become. Over time, it became something much more: the place where my wife and I built our life and started our family.

This is where our kids took their first steps. Where holidays brought family and friends together around the table. Where summer evenings spilled out onto the deck, projects slowly became finished rooms, and more than a decade of ordinary days turned into the memories that make a house feel like home.

As a licensed Washington real estate broker, I spend a lot of time thinking about what makes a home special: the neighborhood, the layout, the character, and the possibilities it offers the people who live there. With this house, I don't have to imagine any of that. I've experienced it firsthand.

So, for me, this isn't simply another property to sell. It's a home my family has loved, improved, grown into, and now gets to pass along to someone new. I'm excited to see who makes it their own and begins the next chapter here, and I'd be honored to show it to you myself.
